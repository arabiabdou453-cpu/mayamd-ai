The comprehension gap

Patients think they understood. Most didn't.

In studies of older adults, nearly everyone says they understand why they were hospitalized, but far fewer can describe their diagnosis. Few recognize their own gaps, and nurses rarely have time to check.

Example patient journey

How Maya explains a medication change.

Robert is going home after a heart-failure stay. His care team changed four medicines. Maya explains the list his clinicians reconciled; it never changes a medication itself.

  • NewDapagliflozin 10 mg, once a dayA new heart medicine that also protects the kidneys. It can make you urinate more.
  • ChangedFurosemide: 20 mg → 40 mg every morningYour water pill went up to keep fluid from building up again.
  • StoppedIbuprofen (Advil, Motrin)It can make your body hold on to fluid. Ask your care team what to use for pain instead.
  • ContinueMetoprolol succinate 50 mg, once a dayNo change. Keep taking it the way you did before.

MAYARobert, your care team made some changes to your medicines. Let's go through them one at a time.

MAYAFirst, you'll stop taking ibuprofen, like Advil or Motrin. It can make your body hold on to fluid, which is hard on your heart.

ROBERTI take Advil for my back. What do I use now?

MAYAGood question. I've flagged it for your care team so they can tell you what's safe for your back pain.

MAYATo make sure I explained it well: in your own words, which medicine are you stopping, and why?

ROBERTNo more Advil, because it makes me keep water.

✓ Teach-back confirmed · question sent to the care team

Symptom triage, built in

A new symptom at home gets a clinical answer, not a default trip to the ER.

During the 30 days, patients can report a new or worsening symptom by app or text at any time. Maya’s built-in symptom triage asks the follow-up questions a clinician would, recommends the right level of care and writes a note for your team. Emergencies are sent to the ER right away. Everything else is routed to your clinicians or a telemedicine visit, so avoidable ER visits and readmissions stay avoidable.

Questions health systems ask

How is this different from AI calling tools?

MayaRED starts at discharge, delivers the full RED protocol with teach-back, and our staff supports your clinicians for 30 days.

Do patients need to download an app?

No. Patients open their discharge conversation from a secure web link on a phone or computer. Patients in 30-day follow-up can also use the Maya companion app.

How is it paid for?

MayaMD bills transitional care management (TCM) directly, so there’s a $0 fee to your health system. If your own clinicians prefer to bill TCM, our fee is paid from that new revenue, not your current budget. Either way, we handle enrollment, documentation and claims prep.

Do we need an EHR integration?

No. The pilot runs on discharge summaries sent by secure email. Read-only EHR access or a real-time ADT feed can be added later.

Does it replace our discharge nurses or RPM program?

No. Maya complements your nurses, case managers and any existing RPM program, and shows them which patients need another conversation.

Where do we start?

One unit or condition, usually heart failure or another CMS-penalized condition. Results review at 90 days.
